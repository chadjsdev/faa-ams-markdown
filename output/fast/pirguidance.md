**1. POST-IMPLEMENTATION REVIEW GUIDANCE**

**1.1 What is a Post-Implementation Review?**

The Post-Implementation Review (PIR) is an evaluation tool used to assess the results of an investment program against baseline expectations once sufficient time has passed after deployment at an operational site.

**1.2 Why Do We Conduct Them?**

We conduct PIRs to examine the differences between estimated and actual investment costs, schedules, benefits, performance, and mission outcomes to answer the questions: “Did we get what we asked for?” and “Are we providing what the customer/user/stakeholder needs?” We answer these questions by assessing whether the investment program is achieving its performance and benefit targets, whether it is meeting the service needs of customers, and whether the business case is still valid.

We also conduct PIRs to determine if the overall investment selection and control process is producing the types of products and systems that help the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) meet its agency-level goals. It is unusual that a single PIR will uncover flaws in the investment decision making process. Therefore, planned periodic reviews of the collective results of PIRs from projects during different stages of their lifecycles are performed to validate and fine-tune the investment decision making process.

**1.3 What Investment Programs Do We Review?**

The FAA conducts PIRs on all investment programs, whether they invest in systems and equipment, facilities, or services, and regardless of line of business, unless the PIR Quality Officer determines that PIR is not applicable due to minimal investment risk or benefit. Post-implementation reviews may also be conducted on related investment programs intended to achieve composite service outcomes, as directed by the Joint Resources Council (JRC) or Director of the performing organization. The JRC may also request a PIR be performed on successful or failed investment programs to determine why they were successful or why they failed, and to identify best practices and lessons learned for general application in the FAA.

**1.4 When Do We Conduct Post-implementation Reviews?**

The FAA conducts PIRs after the first product of an investment program has been operational 6 to 24 months. Typically, the review of mission support and software investment programs occurs within 6 to 18 months of operational service, while the review of National Airspace System (NAS) programs begins no later than two years after declaration of operational service. This allows time for resolution of issues related to premature or early implementation and achievement of operational maturity prior to the review. The Director of the performing organization determines the operational date using standard criteria such as the initial operating condition milestone, the operational readiness decision milestone, the initial daily use milestone, the planned capability achieved milestone, or the date of commissioning, as appropriate to the investment program.

**1.5 Can We Tailor the Review?**

Post-implementation reviews are *always* tailored to the size, complexity, and importance of an investment program or set of programs. Activities and costs are scaled appropriately.

**1.6 What Does the Review Cover?**

The Director of the performing organization determines the content and scope of the PIR for investment programs within the Directorate according to the measures in Table 1. The Director of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, also establish criteria for inclusion in the review appropriate to the nature of investment programs of similar size and nature; e.g., criteria for automation programs of similar size and scope, criteria for communications programs of similar size and scope, etc.

Table 1 defines the measures to be used as the basis for planning, conducting, and reporting the post-implementation review. These measures cover internal business results, performance, strategic initiatives, customer/user satisfaction, benefits, and learned lessons. The measure column in Table 1 shows possible measures the Director of the performing organization might consider as evidence to satisfy a measure. The Director may adjust the measures based on program-specific information. A list of data sources of information for the measures is found in Appendix A.

**Table 1: Measurement Categories/Measures for the Post-Implementation Review**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Measurement Category** | **Measures may include:** |
|
| **1) Business Results** | Facilities and Equipment (F&E) cost results, Operations and Maintenance (O&M) cost results, Acquisition Management System (AMS) milestones, Key Acquisition Program Baseline (APB) or Execution Plan cost and schedule measures |
| **2) Performance** | Technical performance requirements in the Requirements Document (RD) and Reliability, Maintainability and Availability (RMA), Critical Operational Issues (COIs), and safety performance |
| **3) Strategic Initiatives and Service Objectives** | Agency-level goals, objectives of the performing organization, and user/stakeholder objectives |
| **4) Benefits** | Quantitative and qualitative benefit targets in the Business Case (BC) |
| **Conclusions/Lessons Learned** | Conclusions from measurement categories 1-4, as well as impact of environmental or resource changes, procurement process observations, fidelity and stability of requirements, etc. |

**1.7 Who is Responsible?**

The Director of the performing organization for the investment program:

* Establishes measures for the PIR as shown in Table 1 and approves the associated PIR strategy developed by the investment program;
* Finances the PIR;
* Staffs the PIR team;
* Reviews and approves the PIR plan developed by the PIR team;
* Reviews the PIR report received from the PIR team;
* Develops a plan of action and milestones for addressing findings and recommendations of the PIR;
* Coordinates with the Director of the sponsoring organization during the development of the PIR strategy, plan, and plan of action and milestones;
* Provides PIR status at acquisition quarterly program reviews; and
* Closes actions identified in the plan of action and milestones.

The Director of the sponsoring organization for the investment program:

* Reviews and approves the PIR strategy developed by the investment program;
* Reviews and approves the PIR plan developed by the PIR team;
* Reviews the PIR report received from the PIR team; and
* Reviews and approves the plan of action and milestones for addressing findings and recommendations of the PIR.

The PIR team:

* Writes the PIR plan based on measures established by the Director of the performing organization;
* Submits the PIR plan to the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, for approval;
* Conducts the PIR;
* Writes the PIR report;
* Delivers the PIR report to the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, and provides a copy to the PIR Quality Officer; and
* Briefs the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, on the results of the PIR.

The PIR Quality Officer:

* Participates in all PIRs as the agency subject-matter expert to validate whether PIR planning, conduct, and reporting meet agency standards and are independent;
* Verifies the validity of the PIR strategy and plan before submission to the Director(s) for signature;
* Monitors PIR processes across organizations for consistency and quality;
* Plans and conducts periodic reviews of the collective results of PIRs from programs/projects during different stages of their lifecycles to identify and make recommendations to the Acquisition Executive Board and/or the JRC for improvements to, or fine-tuning of, the investment decision making process;
* Works through the Acquisition Executive Board to capture and recommend proposed changes to PIR policy and processes;
* Ensures the provision of PIR training for the PIR team;
* Maintains a repository of PIR strategies, data readiness assessments, plans, reports, and plans of action and milestones for all investment programs;
* Provides a copy of PIR plans, reports, and plans of action and milestones to the JRC Executive Secretariat Support Staff for posting to the JRC Read Ahead site;
* Monitors closure of actions as reported by the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, in the PIR plan of action and milestones;
* Updates the agency-level PIR plan each year with input from the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable; and
* Tracks and monitors the overall portfolio of PIR activities for the agency.

**1.8 Who Performs the Review?**

The Director of the performing organization selects the members of the PIR team necessary to conduct a meaningful and independent PIR. The Director may enlist the services of the PIR Quality Officer to lead the PIR on the Director’s behalf if resources or expertise are not available in the directorate. The PIR team is a cross-functional group of key stakeholders, users, and technical experts who plan the PIR, conduct the PIR, and report results to the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable. Team members may serve more than one function. **Members of the investment program do not participate in the PIR other than to provide data and information required to plan and execute the PIR.**

**1.9 How are the PIRs Planned and Financed?**

The PIR team plans the PIR using the plan template in the FAA Acquisition System Toolset (FAST). The PIR plan template provides directions on how to complete the plan. The PIR team must prepare the PIR plan, the PIR Quality Officer must validate the plan, and the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, must approve the plan prior to conducting the PIR.

The Director of the performing organization funds the PIR and ensures the costs for the PIR are included in the acquisition program baseline of the investment program. The cost of a review must be proportional to the scale of the program.

**Planning for PIRs must be complete prior to starting the PIR.**

**1.10 What Do We Do With Results?**

The PIR team reports results to the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable, using the report template in FAST. The PIR report template provides directions on how to complete the report. The PIR team must prepare the report and the PIR Quality Officer must validate it prior to submission to the Directors of the performing and sponsoring organizations, as applicable.

The Director of the performing organization reviews the report and determines whether there are critical exceptions that need to be reported up the management chain. The Director of the performing organization develops a plan of action and milestones to address the findings and recommendations of the review and coordinates with the Director of the sponsoring organization, as applicable.

The Director of the performing organization reports the status of PIRs at acquisition quarterly program reviews. The report at the acquisition quarterly program review includes:

* Programs in the Director’s purview that underwent a PIR;
* Either confirmation that the results of the PIR were all ‘green’ or;
* The status of the plan of action and milestones (if warranted).

Collective results on multiple programs help the FAA identify ways to improve investment planning and control processes, enable more accurate estimates of investment costs and benefits, and ultimately result in better investment decisions.

**1.11 Tips for a Successful Post-implementation Review**

* Participate in the PIR Data Readiness Assessment process, as required;
* Conduct the PIR against expectations in the acquisition program baseline or execution plan, and business case;
* Consider the following questions when conducting the PIR and reporting results:
  + Did the FAA invest money in the right place?
  + Is the product operating within planned costs?
  + Did product costs fall within planned costs?
  + Did the FAA get what it asked for?
  + Were baseline requirements actually fulfilled?
  + Is the product providing anticipated benefits?
  + Is the product useable and maintainable?
  + Are there lessons learned that can be applied to other investment programs?
  + Did the investment program move the FAA closer to or enable NextGen?
  + Is feedback available from the aviation industry on the effectiveness of the investment program?
  + Did FAA investment and business processes aid or hinder execution of the investment program?
  + Was there early user involvement?
* Ensure issues are handled effectively and that there is a plan for closure;
* Identify next steps clearly; and
* Follow recommendations and actions to completion.
