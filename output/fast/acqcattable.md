# acqcattable

Purpose  

Acquisition categories (ACATs) ensure each FAA investment initiative receives the appropriate level of governance during the planning stages of the AMS lifecycle management process (concept and requirements definition and investment analysis). Acquisition categories apply in the National Airspace System (NAS), as well as to administrative and mission-support initiatives funded by the Facilities and Equipment appropriation. 

ACAT Definitions

Table 1.2.5.1-1 below defines each FAA acquisition category (New Investment, Software Enhancement, Technology Refreshment, Facilities, Variable Quantity, Support Services, Research and Concept Maturity). Designation criteria for determining the appropriate governance level within each category follows the definitions. This table provides a high-level overview of the key artifacts required prior to a JRC decision. For a comprehensive list of required artifacts, please see the JRC Checklist at  HYPERLINK "https://ksn2.faa.gov/afn/jrcportal/Shared%20Documents/JRC%20Readiness%20Criteria%20Checklist.pdf" https://ksn2.faa.gov/afn/jrcportal/Shared%20Documents/JRC%20Readiness%20Criteria%20Checklist.pdf.

ACAT Determination

Early in the concept and requirements definition phase of the AMS lifecycle management process, the service organization or program office sponsoring an investment initiative in response to an approved service need within an FAA Enterprise Architecture Roadmap determines and recommends an ACAT category and level for the initiative based on the definitions and designation criteria in Table 1.2.5.1-1. Guidance for the ACAT Determination Process and Request Form criteria is found at:  HYPERLINK "https://fast.faa.gov/NFFCA\_Acquisition\_Categories.cfm" https://fast.faa.gov/NFFCA\_Acquisition\_Categories.cfm. The ACAT Table Determination Request Form is found at:  HYPERLINK "https://fast.faa.gov/docs/acqcatform.pdf" https://fast.faa.gov/docs/acqcatform.pdf.

ACAT Approval

The service organization or program office sponsoring the investment initiative contacts the Acquisition Executive Board Secretariat to schedule presentation of the ACAT recommendation to the Board for discussion and decision. The Acquisition Executive Board assigns the appropriate acquisition category to the initiative and forwards it to the Joint Resources Council for concurrence. The Acquisition Executive Board revisits the ACAT determination before the investment analysis readiness decision to ensure the designation and level remains appropriate.

Project Level

Facility projects are those individual investments made at a site-level.  A Facility project must review the designation criteria listed in Table 1.2.5.1-13 to determine the appropriate sub-ACAT.  Facility projects may fall into one of three sub-ACATs (F1, F2, and F3).  Facility projects may result in new safety or security implications.

Higher-dollar value projects involving new construction or complex/politically sensitive aspects will be categorized as F1 projects.  For the purposes of AMS, new construction refers to the establishment of a brand new facility, and does not include the replacement or modernization of an existing facility.  Lower-dollar value and/or less complex projects, including replacements or modernizations, will be categorized as F2 and F3 projects.  F1 projects will go through a modified Investment Analysis process, and F2 and F3 projects will follow LOB-specific governance and documentation requirements.

Support Services Contract

|  |
| --- |
| **Definition:** An initiative that includes contracts or agreements (including inter-agency agreements) associated with procuring technical, engineering, scientific, professional, management, and administrative expertise, advice, analysis, studies, or reports.  Support services contracts programs follow the existing contracting guidance in AMS policy. A Support Services Contract program is associated with providing support services for one of the following:     Technical,  engineering, and scientific expertise, advice, analysis, studies, or reports   Professional, management, and administrative expertise, advice, analysis, studies, or reports |

A Support Services Contract must review the designation criteria listed in Table 1.2.5.1-14 to determine the appropriate acquisition category (ACAT). These programs may fall into one of three ACATs which determine the appropriate level of oversight and artifact requirements.  Support Services Contracts will not be required to complete a phased AMS review process, and will not be required to complete Concept and Requirements Definition (CRD), Initial Investment Analysis (IIA), and Final Investment Analysis (FIA).  Support Services Contracts over $10 million (SC ACATs 1 and 2) will be required to complete the Support Contracts Review Board (SCRB) review process.  Support Services Contracts below $10 million (SC ACAT 3) will be required to complete a Line of Business (LOB) review and receive approval from the Contracting Officer.

Table 1.2.5.1-14 outlines the designation criteria, Governance Investment Decision Authority (IDA), reviewing organizations and key artifacts required during the review process for Support Services Contracts.  

Note 1: Service-Gap Analysis and a CRD Readiness Decision is not required for this Investment Type, unless otherwise directed by the JRC.

Note 2: An assessment should be made to determine if safety will be impacted by the Support Services Contract. If there is no impact, state it is not required.  If there is an impact, conduct a safety assessment following the SRMGSA Guidelines.

Note 3: Services for NAS operations, such as flight services, telecommunication, satellite, air traffic control, weather, etc., will typically be characterized as a New Investment rather than a Support Services Contract.  Contact the Concept and Requirements Definition Services Branch (ANG-D23) for help to properly characterize services that provide or affect NAS operations.

Research and Concept Maturity

|  |
| --- |
| Definition: An initiative that supports aviation research on materials, advanced technologies, and human factors to support development of new products, services, and procedures. This includes feasibility studies, technical analyses, prototype demonstrations, and operational assessments that identify, develop, and evaluate potential concepts for improving service delivery by the FAA. Does not apply to New Investment initiatives requiring Category 1 Facilities and Equipment funding in support of concept and requirements definition and investment analysis funding. |

This acquisition category applies only to those research and concept maturity initiatives seeking F&E funding. Initiatives are reviewed by the Acquisition Executive Board which assigns an acquisition category level that is reviewed by the Joint Resources Council.
