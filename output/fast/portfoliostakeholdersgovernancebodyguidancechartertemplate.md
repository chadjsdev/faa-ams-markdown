**Overview**

A Technology Refreshment Portfolio (TRP) acquisition category (ACAT) is intended to help keep a group of inter-related, yet separable fielded products, systems, equipment, and services maintained, sustained, and operational. The program is not expected to improve existing functionality unless it is required to sustain the product/system/equipment. A TRP is typically constituted from multiple previous program investments. Note: ***The TRP ACAT is not appropriate for multiple projects that do not provide a common functionality or capability nor is it appropriate for programs that cannot be divided into discrete sub-projects that can then be prioritized against each other.***

The TRP ACAT:

* May include software and hardware.
* May result in new safety or security implications.
* Allows flexibility in the implementation and prioritization of sustainment programs by bundling a group of similar and related projects together.
* Allows Lines of Business (LOBs) the flexibility to respond to changing conditions, needs, priorities and/or unforeseen environmental factors, such as accelerating failure rates and replacement part availability.
* Enables updates to system technology and ensures continued reliability, maintainability, extensibility and supportability.
* May provide improvements ensuring current and future compliance with software, data, communication standards, and enterprise services. The program has an enterprise-wide impact, whereby a change to the product or system will sustain its continued use for a specified period.

Note: As with any ACAT determination, program teams will make a recommendation about whether their program is a TRP ACAT. The AEB, however, reserves the right to accept or reject the recommendation, and the FAE reserves the right to approve or deny this categorization.

Some investment types are incorporated into the Technology Refreshment Portfolio ACAT, including:

* **<u>Service Life Extension (SLEP):</u>** Insertion of new components, upgrades, modifications, or modernizations to an existing system meant to sustain the current level of service beyond the original service life. Changes may improve system performance but must not add functionality.
* **<u>Replacement-in-Kind:</u>** An as-needed exchange or introduction of a similar or equivalent component or part of a system which satisfies the original design-specification and results in absolutely no change to form, fit, function, specification, performance or procedures in order to sustain the original service life of the planned system.

**Project Level**

Individually managed programs within TRP are considered sub-projects and must be able to be prioritized based on tradeoffs between service shortfalls, funding and other agency needs.

Technology Refreshment projects will fall into one of two sub-projects:

1. **<u>TRP Sub-Project Level I:</u>** A project with a total cost greater than $20M or an overall rating of high for the five key designation criteria: Safety, Security, Risk, Political Sensitivity, and Complexity. Sub-projects in this level will advance through a modified investment analysis process, with a Final Investment Decision before the Joint Resources Council (JRC).
2. **<u>TRP Sub-Project Level II:</u>** A project with a total cost less than $20M or an overall rating of medium/low for the five key designation criteria: Safety, Security, Risk, Political Sensitivity, and Complexity. Level II sub-projects will follow LOB-specific governance and documentation requirements. This group of sub-projects will have Final Investment Decisions before the Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body (SGB); sub-projects will be prioritized and managed by the SGB. The PM is required to contact and coordinate with each of the points of contact for the items listed in the JRC FID checklist in order to determine (if any) needs to be done and what artifacts need to be produced prior to the delegated FID at the SGB.

An Execution Plan and the SGB Charter, both approved by the JRC at the Investment Analysis Readiness Decision, will govern the TRP. A duration of five years is the standard for execution plans. Variations to the five-year period, must be considered on case-by-case basis, and in consultation with Investment Planning and Analysis (AFI-1) and other relevant stakeholders.

**Technology Refreshment Portfolio Management**

Although the Final Investment Decision (FID) for all sub-project Level II resides with the SGB, standard, key elements of AMS Lifecycle Management policy must be followed:

* Service organizations manage fully integrated portfolios of investment and operational assets to optimize service delivery over time
* Portfolio managers coordinate implementation of all materiel and non-materiel investment increments necessary to obtain an operational capability
* Project manager is required to contact and coordinate with each of the points of contact for the items listed on the JRC FID checklist in order to determine (if any) needs to be done and what artifacts need to be produced prior to the delegated FID at the SGB
* Investment decisions, as well as prioritization decisions, are based on the relative merit of different investment opportunities
* Prioritization decisions should be based on factors including: costs, expected benefits, risk, end of service life, maintainability, reliability, criticality of failure, etc.
* Operational performance, costs and benefits are evaluated periodically throughout the portfolio’s performance period
* Knowledge-based decision making, utilizing specific knowledge and defined decision criteria, as determined by the SGB charter, must be utilized

The SGB must institute measurement and analysis processes according to AMS policy and guidance. These measurements and analyses must be the basis for all SGB decisions. The process required include:

* Collect, store, analyze, and report data on seventeen standard measures defined in Standard Program Performance Measures
* Collect, store, analyze, and report baseline performance data defined in the Acquisition Baseline Management Standard Operating Procedure for those programs with an approved acquisition program baseline or execution plan
* Provide early warning indicators of program issues before they become major problems

Measurement and analysis information needs include, but are not limited to:

* Contract information that supports management and executive monitoring of vendor performance;
* Contract information that supports acquisition quality assurance
* Program, operational, risk, and contract information that supports monitoring of lifecycle cost, schedule, performance baselines, as well as benefits and technical progress
* Program information that supports achievement of FAA strategic goals and alignment with the enterprise architecture
* Operational and business case information that supports investment decision-making

**Stakeholder Governance Body Responsibilities**

Upon approval of the TRP ACAT, the program must complete the SGB Charter (Appendix A.) This is an AMS required document, and is required prior to an Investment Analysis Readiness Decision before the JRC. The chairperson of the SGB should be the representative from the Service Organization with need. Each member of the body must identify a standing representative to attend in his or her absence. Participation in the governance body requires each member to review materials sent prior to each meeting and come prepared to discuss issues, engage in the process, and render acquisition and prioritization decision when required.

The chairperson of the SGB is a manager in the Service Organization implementing the portfolio. The chair is responsible to ensure necessary review materials are provided to all members prior to each SGB meeting and that records of decisions are developed, finalized and retained.

The chair must ensure that all SGB prioritization actions, delegated final investment decisions and procedures are in compliance with the SGB charter as well as AMS policy.  The chair must ensure that proper outreach and coordination is conducted with all relevant stakeholders for each sub-project within the portfolio.

While this charter will define the standards, guidelines and practices the SGB will follow, all activities must follow AMS policy to facilitate sound governance and judicious use of funds. The SGB will make a Final Investment Decision for each sub-project investment within their authority. All sub-projects must have a final investment decision. Sub-project Level II will have the SGB as the investment decision authority that makes that final investment decision. SGB is the final decision authority for all sub-project Level II. While the process to reach this decision is determined in the SGB Charter, the program office must coordinate with each stakeholder on the JRC point of contact list to obtain concurrence prior to any Final Investment Decision. A consensus decision-making model is the basis for all decisions. Should the SGB be unable to reach consensus, the decision is referred to the JRC for a decision.

The SGB is responsible for creating records of each decision, both for any Final Investment Decision, as well as for all prioritization decisions. This should include any measurement and analysis work justifying the decision. The SGB must define, and codify in its charter, how it will monitor its cost, schedule, performance, and progress as projects are executed and implemented. Depending on the nature of an individual project (e.g. sub-ACAT determination, cost, technical complexity, etc.), the processes used may vary, but any variances should be based on compelling reasoning. FID process may vary from prioritization process but all delegated FID decision should be consistent with each other. For prioritization decisions, the body must create and document forms to record the prioritization discussions, the prioritization decisions made, and the justification for those decisions. This documentation must also be of sufficient detail to provide justification for the body’s decisions.

The documents used to reach decisions (both delegated FID as well as prioritization decisions), all meeting minutes, and any presentations made to the governance body must be forwarded to the JRC Secretariat for record retention. The Governance Body chairperson must sign the meeting minutes, and any Records of Decision, prior to providing to the JRC Secretariat. Performance metrics for the portfolio must be included in the documentation given to the JRC Secretariat for retention.

The SGB must manage its portfolio of sub-projects during solution implementation within execution plans approved at the IARD. They must take action to correct negative variance from any cost, schedule, or performance baseline measure. Negative variances that exceed 10 percent must be reported quarterly to the Joint Resources Council, along with an explanation of the cause(s), impact on service delivery, and a recovery strategy. The Administrator must notify the Congress of any program cost or schedule variance exceeding 50 percent and must either terminate the activity or justify why it should be continued and provide a recovery plan. When the Joint Resources Council determines an investment program cannot recover from a degenerating negative baseline variance, it may elect to re-baseline the effort by adding resources or changing its scope or schedule, or it may decide to terminate the activity.

**Introduction/Purpose**

Define/Describe the Program. This section should include a brief description of the Technology Refreshment Portfolio program, its purpose, and history. It should also include a description of the system(s) that will be maintained and a high-level description of the program management process, practices and work products.

**Scope**

Define the scope of the Charter. This includes defining the policies, standards, guidelines and practices that are applicable to the management of the program. The authority of the charter members and responsible organizations should be defined here.

**Membership**

This section defines the roles of organizations and their representative members who comprise the Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body. The membership is not limited to the line of business or staff office associated with the Service Organization with Need. Membership may include other LOBs and staff offices with an interest in the portfolio, as well as external stakeholder organizations. These external organizations could include the following:

* Technical Training
* Systems Engineering
* Test and Evaluation
* Logistics
* Safety & Security
* Technical Operations
* AJI
* ASH

If organizations elect not to participate, the SGB should use the JRC checklist to facilitate coordination with stakeholder organizations. The SGB must employ the same coordination process with the stakeholder organizations required in the FID section of Technology Refreshment sub-Project Level 1 found on the JRC Checklist. The JRC Secretariat and the JRC POC list can provide guidance.

It should detail the program hierarchy including groups or offices with the authority for disposition of decisions and issues, particularly as they apply to approvals and escalations, such as to the JRC.

**Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body Decision Making Process**

This section should include the process by which the Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body makes decisions. This process should be based on description of how the body works with other key organizations, including programs dependent on systems within the portfolio, should be included here.

**Charter Governance**

Describe the governance strategy for the charter. This can include rules for updates, periodicity of review, and approval authorities. The processes defined here should be broad enough in scope to cover changes to any part of the charter.

**Portfolio Management Overview**

Define the specifics about the Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body operating procedures. There should be descriptions of the analysis and prioritization, projection investment decision, and program control processes the program intends to follow for the Program.

**Sustainability Analysis and Projection Prioritization Processes**

The Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body should describe the method by and frequency with which they will update the sustainability analyses for the portfolio of systems, including diminishing manufacturing sources and material shortages (DMSMS), end of support, and reliability issues. It must also designate the factors determining the prioritization of projects within the Program.

**Decision Processes and Documentation Requirements**

The Portfolio Stakeholder Governance Body should express how it would review various projects under the portfolio.

For Final Investment Decisions (sub-ACAT Project II) the governance body must create and document the process and criteria used to determine when a project is ready to present the request for a delegated FID, and gain approval to enter Solution Implementation and Deployment. It must designate the documentation required to make those decisions. The documentation needs to be of sufficient detail to provide justification for resulting investment decisions.

**Performance Monitoring and Reporting**

The portfolio should define how it will monitor its cost, schedule, performance, and progress as projects are executed. Depending on the nature of an individual project (e.g. sub-ACAT determination, cost, technical complexity, etc.), the processes used may vary, but any variances should be based on compelling reasoning. All delegated FID decisions should be consistent with each other.

Procedures for reporting on performance metrics should also be defined in this section. Metrics must include, at a minimum, cost, schedule and performance measures and should be similar to what is in SPIRE. The reporting process should include who is responsible for each step of the process, and under what conditions the process applies.

**Appendices**

Appendices contain details that are not included in the plan itself or that are referenced in the plan. The following appendices are required. Other appendices are at the discretion of the program but may include forms, process maps, reports, metrics such as change requests, deviations, etc.

Provide a list of included sub-projects comprising the Portfolio. Include a project description, program cost estimate, Aggregate Designation Criteria Rating from ACAT designation Form, and assigned project level.
